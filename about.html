About this project

What are we building?

DigiTales AI is a proposed learning platform that uses natural language processing to analyse learners’ open-ended responses and personalise story pathways, feedback, and reflection.

The brief describes indicators such as empathy, risk perception, self-regulation, and critical judgement. Learners would apply their understanding through real-world challenges involving online safety, critical evaluation, ethical decisions, and responsible AI use.

Culturally responsive and multilingual storytelling is also part of the broad brief. The exact feature coverage and implementation are still being defined.

The essentials from the handbook

Full handbook ↗

A year-long group project covering requirements, design, implementation, testing, documentation, and teamwork. There are no written examinations.

AssessmentGroup marks, individual reflection, and peer assessment

Collective group mark

Interim group report20%
Final group report30%
Software20%
Open Day15%
Presentation Day15%

Your overall mark

80% individual mark for group work
20% individual report

Peer assessment redistributes the collective group mark according to individual contributions. One confidential assessment is required in autumn and another in spring, with written justifications for every other member.

It considers research, creative input, cooperation, communication, concrete contributions, and attendance. The supervisor and convenor can review or override problematic ratings.

Higher marks depend on clear requirements, precise design, complete and well-written software, systematic testing, supporting documentation, and professional project management. The individual report is assessed primarily on reflective quality, writing, structure, and presentation.

Reports & submissionWord limits, formats, naming, and deadlines

Interim group report

4,000–5,000 words

Expanded problem and research; agreed requirements; initial system and interface design; justified implementation decisions; early prototype / mini-Scrum results; problems and time plan.

Joint PDF · around halfway through the project.

Final group report

7,000–8,000 words

A self-contained account of purpose, client, scope, literature, methodology, updated design, implementation, testing, component ownership, results against requirements, and technical / teamwork reflection.

Include testing evidence, all formal minutes or a link to the complete set, and a user manual if appropriate. Do not append the interim report.

Joint PDF · at the end of the project.

Individual report

2,000–2,500 words

Your contribution, honest self-assessment, achievements and omissions, lessons learned, and what you would do differently. Contribution volume is addressed by peer assessment.

Individual PDF · at the end of the project.

Rules to remember

  • Word counts exclude the cover / title page, table of contents, references, and appendices. Every 100 words above the upper limit incurs a 5% penalty relative to the report’s total marks.
  • Use the starter-pack cover template: prescribed report title, project title, date, group number, members’ names and usernames, and supervisor. Individual covers identify you and list the other members.
  • Submit on Moodle by 5:00 PM on the actual due date. One member submits joint work; each member submits their own individual report and peer assessments. Late submissions incur 5% per working day under the handbook.
  • Submit developed source code with the final report as ZIP or gzipped TAR. Attribute group-authored, adapted, and external components. Run instructions and a runnable package are encouraged where feasible.
  • Exact deadlines are on Moodle. The filename templates and worked examples conflict about “Grp” and number padding—confirm the required names before submitting.

Teamwork & project practiceMeetings, ownership, records, and the prototype
  • Appoint a group leader to coordinate fair work, progress, and submissions. The project and its decisions belong to the whole group; the supervisor provides advice.
  • Meet internally every week. Supervisor meetings are normally weekly or biweekly, around 30 minutes, with rotating chair and secretary roles. Preserve all formal minutes.
  • Agree industry-client meetings monthly or once / twice per semester. For this project, Dr Regina coordinates readiness and preparation but will not attend the client meeting.
  • Expect about 200 hours over the module, averaging roughly 9 hours per term-time week. Use version control, document interfaces, integrate early, and draft reports throughout the project.
  • Aim for a working prototype by late November, then refine it using feedback. This is a handbook target, not an exact submission deadline.
  • Groups are allocated; forming your own group or swapping members is not permitted. Allocation or contact problems should go to the convenor.
Research & equipmentEthics review and requests for non-standard resources
  • All groups must discuss ethics with the supervisor. Research involving human participants, non-public personal data, or biological materials requires one group ethics application.
  • Use the Moodle form, work with the supervisor, submit it before relevant activities start, and revise it if it does not meet requirements.
  • Identify non-standard hardware or software needs in the first few weeks. Only one coordinated request per project is permitted, via supervisor approval. Availability is not guaranteed.
  • Students must not install hardware or software on school machines themselves.
The final showcaseOpen Day and Presentation Day

Open Day

A professional stall with posters, leaflets, and a working live demonstration for students, staff, and industry guests. Assessment covers demonstration reliability, display quality, and the team’s ability to explain the project.

Presentation Day

A 10-minute presentation followed by 5 minutes of questions. Explain the problem, application, and reflective conclusions. Preferably involve all members. Presentation quality and handling questions are assessed.

Pre-prepared demonstration slides are recommended. Check the schedule issued one week beforehand and arrive during the preceding break to prepare.

Dress formally for both events. Event dates and arrangements are on Moodle.
